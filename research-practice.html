Research practice

The four-step method, explained.

This is the framework the prompts are built on. Careful research runs through four steps, each resting on the one before, with a set of standards that hold the whole way through. Read it to understand what the prompts are doing and why.

The steps are not strictly one-way. Something found late can send you back to redo an earlier part. Each step below lists the methods it involves; where a method also has a prompt you can run on its own, that is noted, and the prompts themselves live in the prompt library.

01Frame

Frame the topic

Turn a topic into a question you can actually research, before any searching starts. An AI will not question a badly framed topic; it answers what it is given. A researcher works out what decision the research serves and what a useful answer looks like first, and often finds the question worth answering sits just to one side of the one that was asked.

Methods in this step
Restate the question
Rewrite the topic as three things: the decision it informs, the questions you would need to answer to make it, and the evidence that would answer them. A topic that cannot be rewritten this way is not yet ready to research.
Has a prompt
Check the framing
Say what nearby or better question the topic points to, and what the version as asked might miss.
Part of the step
Place the effort
Mark which lines of enquiry are background and which the conclusion will hang on, so the deep work goes where it matters.
Part of the step
02Gather

Gather and verify

Collect material and work out what can be trusted, one item at a time. This is where an AI on its own falls furthest short: it reads the top few results and stops. The methods here keep it chasing details to sources that can actually support them, and recording what has and has not been confirmed.

Methods in this step
Spot the lead
Notice the details worth following up, even in a poor source: a named person, a document referred to but not linked, a number that conflicts with another, and things that should be present and are missing.
Has a prompt
Chase the detail
Take a checkable specific out of a weak source and confirm it against one that can actually stand behind it. A weak source is a lead, not evidence.
Has a prompt
Resolve and record claims
Keep a running record of every claim that matters, its status, and its source, so nothing is silently asserted or silently dropped.
Has a prompt
Know when to stop
A floor and a ceiling: do not rest anything important on a single source when a second could be found, and after three failed searches for a detail, mark it unconfirmed and move on.
Part of the step
03Analyse

Analyse

Produce findings that are not sitting in any single source. This is what separates real analysis from a summary. The core move is combination: putting two things side by side and reading what comes out of the comparison.

Methods in this step
Put two patterns side by side
Lay one thing against another in a table and read the shape of the result, not the individual cells: where things spread, bunch, crowd or leave gaps, and what that implies.
Has a prompt
Read the differences
When the two are peers, the finding is in where they differ: what each treats as central, where one is specific and the other vague or silent.
Part of the step
Check the fit
If most of one will not line up against the other, the finding is that they do not belong together. Say so and suggest a better pairing rather than forcing it.
Part of the step
04Conclude

Conclude

Test the finding and put it in a form you can trust. Search for evidence against the conclusion, state what the checked material actually supports, and show which claims it rests on.

Methods in this step
Look for evidence against the conclusion
Once you have a conclusion, search for evidence that it is wrong, worded to find the opposite, and deal with the strongest objection you find.
Has a prompt
Build the finding
State what the checked material actually supports, which is not always the direction most of the material seems to point.
Part of the step
Show what it rests on
The conclusion names the specific claims it depends on, and each of those has been confirmed.
Part of the step
Test the draft
Check a finished piece against the standards: every fact traceable to a good source, anything unconfirmed marked as such, the contrary case addressed.
Has a prompt
Bedrock / holds throughout

Standards that run under every step

ST-1Rank the sourcesPrimary and institutional sources support a fact; weak sources only point to leads.
ST-2Match effort to stakesBackground gets one source; a load-bearing claim gets the full chase.
ST-3Keep a running recordEvery claim that matters, its status, its best source.
ST-4RevisitA finding that undoes earlier work sends the task back.
